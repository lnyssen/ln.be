Inclusive design is not a box I check at the end of a project. It is the very foundation where my process begins. Far from limiting creative expression, true constraints force us to think deeper, push past lazy defaults and find genuinely innovative solutions. When you embrace accessibility from day one, those parameters do not restrict your vision. They sharpen it and serve as a catalyst for clearer and more intentional creativity.

## Beyond the last-minute checklist

In most web projects, accessibility arrives too late. It creeps in as a last-minute technical check or an anxious reaction right before launch, where contrast is hastily adjusted and a few image descriptions are added in a rush. Handled this way, it feels like a punitive constraint or an administrative checklist that dulls the original creative vision.

## Adapting through creative frustration

My perspective continues to evolve in my current role at Media Animation, where I confront accessible design head-on every day. Early on, navigating strict contrast ratios, complex semantic structures and rigid readability rules felt as though technical boundaries were stifling artistic freedom. Overcoming that friction changed everything. Adapting to those parameters showed me that working within constraints does not restrict my ideas, but makes them stronger, clearer and far more purposeful.

## A powerful engine for invention

This is a misunderstanding. When integrated from the very first sketch, accessibility is not a corrective filter, but a powerful engine for invention. It extracts us from our design habits to ask the fundamental question of our craft. How will our creation actually come to life in the hands of real human beings with diverse backgrounds, bodies and contexts?

## Designing for the margins benefits everyone

Designing for inclusivity is not about catering to an average user who does not exist, but about paying genuine attention to those at the margins. By solving a problem for someone with a disability, we quietly improve the experience for everyone else. High contrast supports the vision of a visually impaired person, but it also saves the reading experience for anyone using their phone in direct sunlight. Thoughtfully designed keyboard navigation is essential for some, while becoming a seamless pleasure for power users who rely on shortcuts. Generous tap targets and crystal-clear copy assist a neurodivergent person just as much as a parent navigating one-handed while holding a baby. Accessibility transforms abstract empathy into tangible design choices.

## Building inclusive design systems and leveraging AI

For this approach to elevate a project, it must be structured from the ground up. Embedding inclusivity directly into a design system ensures that every foundational decision, from accessible color pairings to focus states and motion alternatives, scales effortlessly across the entire product. Artificial intelligence serves as a powerful partner in this workflow by testing contrast ratios early, auditing screen-reader structures in real time and drafting initial alternative text. Yet AI remains an assistant. The overarching creative direction, systemic structure and human empathy are entirely guided by the designer.

## From legal mandate to creative opportunity

In Belgium, this care for others has also become a legal framework. Public institutions have been subject to these standards for a long time, and since June 2025, the European Accessibility Act mandates that key private sector services from e-commerce to banking open their digital doors to everyone. Beyond the law, embracing these principles from the very first line on paper is a unique creative opportunity: the chance to design richer, more durable and deeply human web experiences.

## Design as a dialogue

Ultimately, accessible design is not about compliance or perfection. It is about connection. When we view design as an ongoing dialogue between form, technology and human experience, inclusivity ceases to be a restriction and becomes our greatest creative asset. By listening closely to how different people navigate the world, we build digital spaces that are not only usable for everyone, but undeniably better for all.
